Our Story: Why Divine Leads Media Was Founded
For years, Nigerian schools have faced the same persistent obstacles: teachers spending three to four weeks every term manually calculating student continuous assessment scores and broadsheets by hand; computer rooms with non-working systems or no backup power; and students graduating without knowing how to comfortably type on a keyboard or use modern technology.

Divine Leads Media was founded by Eesuola Joshua Damilare with a clear conviction: technology should make running a school simpler, not more complicated. Instead of building expensive online portals that fail the moment internet or electricity drops, we build practical solutions that work in real Nigerian classrooms.

Today, DLM operates as a cohesive unit uniting four critical parts: conducting field research to understand school needs, developing reliable offline school software like DLM ResultDesk, supplying schools with durable computer hardware and solar power, and equipping learners with digital literacy and AI study skills.
The 4 Cohesive Pillars (Detailed)
1. Classroom & Pedagogical Research
Before we write a single line of software code or recommend equipment, our team visits schools and interviews headteachers, proprietors, and classroom teachers. We examine the exact bottlenecks: how long it takes to process exam scores, what causes errors on report cards, and how power outages disrupt ICT classes. Our research ensures our tools solve real Nigerian problems.
2. Practical Software Development
Our flagship software, DLM ResultDesk, was engineered to solve the most painful administrative bottleneck in Nigerian schools: terminal results computation. We build software that works 100% offline, requires no monthly internet subscriptions, runs on everyday Windows computers, and follows official Nigerian grading standards (WAEC, NECO, BECE).
3. Supplies of Education Technology Equipments
Software needs reliable hardware to run. Many schools have been sold fragile or unsuitable computers that fail within months. Divine Leads Media supplies rugged, tested desktop computers, commercial-grade network cabling for CBT exam centers, and solar/inverter power backup systems configured specifically for Nigerian climate and power realities.
4. Digital & AI Skills for Nigerian Learners
A well-equipped computer lab is useless without skilled students. We run structured, practical workshops for primary and secondary school pupils. We do not teach theoretical concepts; we teach hands-on typing, computer operating skills, productive internet research, and how to safely prompt AI tools to understand complex subjects in mathematics, basic science, and literature.
Why We Built DLM ResultDesk
At the end of every school term in Nigeria, teachers are exhausted. They sit for hours with calculators, tallying CA scores, writing out broadsheet rolls on cardboard papers, and copying grades onto individual report cards. A single addition mistake can skew a child's class position and cause disputes with parents.

We built DLM ResultDesk to eliminate this burden. With ResultDesk, a teacher enters each student's score once. The software instantly computes total scores, calculates class positions, generates subject ranks, applies the correct grade letter and remarks, and produces clean, printable PDF report cards with your school logo. What used to take two weeks now takes fifteen minutes.

Guide 1: How to Set Up a Reliable 30-Computer CBT Lab on a School Budget
Tag: CBT & Lab Setup
Author: Babatunde Ojo (Lead, Equipment Supplies)
Publication Date: September 8, 2026
Read Time: 5 min read
Summary: Practical advice for Nigerian school proprietors on buying durable desktop computers, avoiding fragile laptops, and planning proper room cabling.
Full Content:
Setting up a computer-based testing (CBT) lab is one of the best investments a Nigerian school can make, but many proprietors waste money on fragile equipment that breaks down within months. Here are four practical guidelines to follow:

1. Prefer Desktop Computers Over Laptops for Student Labs:
While laptops seem attractive because of built-in batteries, secondary school students easily damage laptop hinges, spill liquids on keyboards, and damage delicate power ports. Sturdy desktop computers with separate keyboards and monitors last much longer. If a student spills water on a desktop keyboard, replacing the keyboard costs less than ₦3,500, whereas replacing a laptop keyboard can cost ₦25,000 or more.

2. Invest in Structured Wired LAN (Ethernet), Not Just Wi-Fi:
Wi-Fi networks become congested when 30 or 50 students attempt to submit exam answers simultaneously. High-grade CAT6 Ethernet cables connected to gigabit network switches provide instant, lag-free examination connections that never drop during mock tests.

3. Protect Systems with Inverters and Surge Protectors:
Never connect school computers directly to raw public power or unstable generators. A central pure sine wave inverter with surge protectors absorbs voltage spikes and keeps your examination running smoothly even when the national grid cuts out.

4. Choose Sturdy Computer Desks with Cable Trays:
Keep cables hidden behind tables to prevent students from tripping on power cords and crashing monitors to the floor.
Guide 2: Why Nigerian Schools Should Stop Hand-Writing Student Report Cards
Tag: School Software
Author: Eesuola Joshua Damilare (Founder & Lead Technologist)
Publication Date: August 19, 2026
Read Time: 6 min read
Summary: Manual calculations waste hundreds of teacher hours, cause math errors, and delay end-of-term vacations. Here is why offline software is the answer.
Full Content:
At the end of every school term, an exhausted calm falls over staff rooms across Nigeria. Teachers sit surrounded by stacks of notebooks, calculators, red pens, and blank cardboard sheets, calculating totals, finding averages, assigning class positions, and copying marks onto paper report cards.

Here are three reasons why this manual system hurts schools:

1. Mathematical Errors Cause Unnecessary Disputes with Parents:
When a tired teacher calculates 12 subjects for 45 students by hand, addition errors are inevitable. A simple mistake of 10 marks can alter a student's position from 3rd to 7th, leading to angry parent confrontations and damaging the school's reputation.

2. Wasted Teacher Hours Could Be Spent Teaching:
Teachers spend up to 40 hours per term simply calculating marks and rewriting student remarks. When software handles calculations, teachers can use that time to prepare lesson notes, assist struggling pupils, or rest before the next term begins.

3. Physical Paper Cards Get Lost or Altered:
Handwritten paper sheets are easily damaged by rain, misplaced, or forged. Offline software like DLM ResultDesk saves permanent digital records that can be reprinted in seconds five or ten years later.

The solution does not require expensive monthly internet subscriptions. An offline tool installed on a school laptop does all the work without consuming internet data.
Guide 3: Practical Ways to Teach AI Literacy to Secondary School Pupils
Tag: AI & Learner Skills
Author: Maryam Bello (Lead Facilitator, Learner Skills)
Publication Date: August 2, 2026
Read Time: 4 min read
Summary: Artificial intelligence is not just for software developers. Here is how Nigerian teachers can guide secondary school students to use AI tools for learning.
Full Content:
Many educators worry that artificial intelligence (AI) will encourage students to cheat on homework. However, when taught properly, AI can be the most patient personal tutor a Nigerian student has ever had.

Here is how schools can introduce AI literacy responsibly:

1. Teach Students How to Ask Good Questions (Prompting):
Show students how to ask AI: 'Explain photosynthesis as if I were in JSS 2, and give me three real-life Nigerian examples.' This teaches children how to articulate clear questions and dissect complex academic topics.

2. Teach Verification and Fact-Checking:
AI models sometimes give inaccurate information. Have students cross-reference AI answers with their approved Nigerian textbooks (such as Stan Biology or New General Mathematics) to spot errors and verify facts.

3. Emphasize Academic Honesty:
Teach students that using AI to explain a difficult concept is smart learning, but copying AI text into an assignment without understanding it deprives them of real knowledge.
Guide 4: Solving School Power Failures with Solar Inverters for Computer Labs
Tag: Power & Infrastructure
Author: Babatunde Ojo (Equipment Supplies Lead)
Publication Date: July 15, 2026
Read Time: 5 min read
Summary: How to calculate your school's computer lab power needs, select reliable inverters, and avoid wasting money on weak batteries.
Full Content:
Nigeria's public power supply is unpredictable, and frequent generator fuel costs quickly strain a school's operating budget. For a computer lab or CBT center, sudden blackouts are especially dangerous because they can wipe out ongoing exam data and burn desktop power supplies.

A properly sized solar inverter system provides a silent, automatic, and cost-effective power shield:

1. Calculate Your Exact Power Load:
Modern energy-efficient desktop computers with LED monitors consume approximately 65 to 80 watts each. A 30-seater lab therefore requires around 2,400 watts of active power. A 5kVA/48V pure sine wave inverter is the ideal capacity for this size of lab.

2. Choose Pure Sine Wave Inverters, Never Modified Sine Wave:
Modified sine wave inverters produce choppy electricity that overheats computer power supplies and causes desktop fans to buzz loudly. Always insist on pure sine wave inverters to protect delicate electronics.

3. Consider Solar Hybrid Systems:
By connecting solar panels to the inverter, the system charges directly from sunlight during daytime classes, reducing both generator fuel consumption and electric bills.
